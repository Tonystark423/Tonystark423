# Corporate Banking Resolution

> **Comprehensive Guide & Document for Opening Business Bank Accounts**

---

## 📌 Overview

This document provides a complete Corporate Banking Resolution template that banks require when opening accounts for LLCs, corporations, and other business entities. It includes detailed explanations of each section to help you understand what banks are looking for.

---

## 📋 Complete Corporate Banking Resolution

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                         CORPORATE BANKING RESOLUTION                          │
│                                                                             │
│  This document certifies that the following individuals are authorized to    │
│  open and operate bank accounts on behalf of the company.                   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

### Section 1: Company Identification

**Purpose:** Establishes the legal identity of your business entity.

```
RESOLVED, that [COMPANY LEGAL NAME], a [STATE OF ORGANIZATION] [ENTITY TYPE: 
LLC/Corporation/Partnership] organized and existing under the laws of the State 
of [STATE], with its principal office located at [PRINCIPAL ADDRESS], and 
possessing Federal Employer Identification Number [EIN], does hereby adopt this 
resolution.
```

**What Banks Verify:**
- ✅ Company name matches Articles of Organization/Incorporation
- ✅ State of organization is correct
- ✅ EIN is valid (IRS CP 575 letter)
- ✅ Principal address matches business records

---

### Section 2: Authorization to Open Bank Accounts

**Purpose:** Grants explicit authority to open and maintain bank accounts.

```
RESOLVED FURTHER, that the following named persons, whose names, titles, and 
specimen signatures appear below, are hereby authorized, severally and not jointly,
to open, maintain, and operate one or more bank accounts (including but not limited 
to checking accounts, savings accounts, money market accounts, and certificates of 
deposit) in the name of the Company with any banking institution or institutions 
selected by the authorized persons.
```

**Key Points:**
- "Severally and not jointly" = Each person can act independently (single signature authority)
- "Any banking institution" = Can open accounts at multiple banks
- Covers all account types banks typically offer

---

### Section 3: Authorized Signatories

**Purpose:** Identifies who can sign on behalf of the company.

```
AUTHORIZED SIGNATORIES:

Name: _________________________    Title: _________________________
Specimen Signature: _________________    Date: _______________

Name: _________________________    Title: _________________________
Specimen Signature: _________________    Date: _______________

Name: _________________________    Title: _________________________
Specimen Signature: _________________    Date: _______________
```

**Bank Requirements:**
- Each signatory must provide a specimen signature
- Titles must match corporate records (Member, Manager, President, etc.)
- Most banks allow 2-4 signatories on a resolution
- Additional signatories may require separate documentation

---

### Section 4: Banking Powers and Authority

**Purpose:** Defines exactly what authorized signatories can do with company accounts.

```
RESOLVED FURTHER, that each of the authorized signatories named above shall have
the full power and authority, on behalf of and in the name of the Company, to:

a)  Deposit, withdraw, and transfer funds;

b)  Sign, endorse, execute, and deliver all checks, drafts, notes, acceptances, 
    bills of exchange, letters of credit, and other negotiable instruments;

c)  Borrow money and execute promissory notes, loan agreements, security 
    agreements, and other evidence of indebtedness;

d)  Overdraw any account and execute overdraft agreements;

e)  Open and close accounts, including but not limited to demand deposit accounts, 
    time deposit accounts, savings accounts, and any other type of account;

f)  Receive statements, notices, and other communications from the bank;

g)  Execute stop payment orders and deposit endorsements;

h)  Use all banking services including but not limited to wire transfers, ACH 
    transactions, safe deposit boxes, credit cards, and merchant services;

i)  Appoint, remove, or replace any agent, proxy, or attorney-in-fact for the 
    purpose of executing any of the foregoing powers;

j)  Execute any and all documents, agreements, or instruments necessary or 
    convenient to carry out any of the foregoing powers.
```

**What Each Power Means:**

| Power | What It Allows | Bank Perspective |
|-------|----------------|------------------|
| (a) Deposit/Withdraw/Transfer | Basic account operations | Standard requirement |
| (b) Sign negotiable instruments | Sign checks, drafts | Required for check writing |
| (c) Borrow money | Take out loans, lines of credit | Required for lending |
| (d) Overdraw accounts | Overdraft protection | Risk management |
| (e) Open/Close accounts | Account management | Flexibility |
| (f) Receive communications | Get statements, notices | Privacy compliance |
| (g) Stop payments | Cancel checks | Fraud prevention |
| (h) All banking services | Wire, ACH, cards | Full service access |
| (i) Appoint agents | Delegate authority | Flexibility |
| (j) Execute documents | Any necessary paperwork | Legal completeness |

---

### Section 5: Signature Authority Definition

**Purpose:** Clarifies how many signatures are required for transactions.

```
RESOLVED FURTHER, that any one (1) of the authorized signatories named above 
shall have the authority to sign any and all instruments, documents, or 
communications on behalf of the Company, and the signature of any one (1) 
authorized signatory shall be binding upon the Company and shall be conclusive 
evidence of the authority of such signatory to act on behalf of the Company.
```

**Signature Authority Options:**

| Type | Description | Pros | Cons |
|------|-------------|------|------|
| **Single Signature** | Any one signatory can act | Fast, convenient | Less control |
| **Joint Signature (2)** | Any two signatories required | More security | Slower |
| **All Signatures** | All signatories required | Maximum security | Very slow |

**Bank Note:** Most banks default to single signature authority unless specified otherwise.

---

### Section 6: Ratification and Indemnification

**Purpose:** Protects the bank and confirms all actions are authorized.

```
RESOLVED FURTHER, that all acts performed by any authorized signatory in 
accordance with this resolution are hereby ratified and confirmed as the acts of 
the Company, and the Company hereby agrees to indemnify and hold harmless any bank 
or financial institution from any and all claims, demands, actions, or causes of 
action arising out of or related to any acts or omissions of any authorized signatory 
acting within the scope of this resolution.
```

**Why This Matters:**
- **Ratification:** Confirms company accepts responsibility for authorized actions
- **Indemnification:** Protects bank from liability if signatory acts properly
- **Legal Protection:** Standard clause that banks require

---

### Section 7: Certification by Company Officer

**Purpose:** Official certification that the resolution is valid.

```
CERTIFICATION

I, [NAME], [TITLE], of [COMPANY NAME], do hereby certify that:

1.  I am duly authorized to execute this resolution on behalf of the Company;
2.  The foregoing resolutions were duly adopted by the Company;
3.  The persons named as authorized signatories are the persons so authorized 
    and their signatures appearing above are genuine;
4.  This resolution has not been altered, modified, or revoked and is in full 
    force and effect as of the date hereof.

IN WITNESS WHEREOF, I have hereunto set my hand this _____ day of _____________, 20____.

____________________________________
[NAME], [TITLE]

STATE OF [STATE]
COUNTY OF [COUNTY]

On this _____ day of _____________, 20____, before me, the undersigned notary 
public, personally appeared [NAME], known to me to be the [TITLE] of [COMPANY 
NAME], and acknowledged that he/she executed the foregoing instrument as the free 
act and deed of said Company.

____________________________________
Notary Public

My Commission Expires: _____________
Notary Seal: _____________________
```

**Certification Requirements:**
- Must be signed by a company officer (President, Manager, Member)
- Must be notarized (most banks require this)
- Notary must verify identity of the signer
- Notary seal must be affixed (if state requires)

---

### Section 8: Bank Use Section

**Purpose:** Space for the bank to record account details.

```
FOR BANK USE ONLY:

Bank Name: _________________________________
Account Number: _____________________________
Account Type: _______________________________
Date Opened: ________________________________
Authorized Bank Representative: ________________

Bank Seal/Stamp: _____________________________
```

## 📁 Required Supporting Documents

When submitting your Corporate Banking Resolution, banks typically require:

### ✅ **Mandatory Documents**
1. **Certified Articles of Organization/Incorporation** - From your Secretary of State
2. **EIN Confirmation Letter (IRS CP 575)** - Proof of Federal Tax ID
3. **Certificate of Good Standing** - From your Secretary of State (usually < 90 days old)
4. **Operating Agreement/Bylaws** - Shows ownership and management structure

### ⚠️ **Commonly Required Documents**
1. **DBA/Fictitious Name Filing** - If operating under a trade name
2. **Business License** - Local business registration
3. **SS-4 Form** - EIN application (sometimes requested)
4. **Ownership Structure Chart** - For complex entities

### 📋 **Document Checklist**

- [ ] Corporate Banking Resolution (signed and notarized)
- [ ] Certified Articles of Organization/Incorporation
- [ ] EIN Confirmation Letter (IRS CP 575)
- [ ] Certificate of Good Standing
- [ ] Operating Agreement or Bylaws
- [ ] DBA Filing (if applicable)
- [ ] Business License (if applicable)
- [ ] Photo ID for each signatory (driver's license, passport)

## 📝 Step-by-Step Process

### Before You Start
1. **Verify your company is in good standing** - Check with your Secretary of State
2. **Confirm your EIN** - Locate your IRS CP 575 letter
3. **Review your Operating Agreement** - Confirm authorized signatories
4. **Check bank requirements** - Call your chosen bank

### Completing the Resolution
1. **Fill in all bracketed fields** with your company information
2. **List all authorized signatories** with their titles
3. **Have each signatory provide a specimen signature**
4. **Define signature authority** (single, joint, or all)
5. **Review all powers** and remove any not needed

### Execution
1. **Print on company letterhead** (if available)
2. **Sign in front of a notary public**
3. **Affix company seal** (if your company has one)
4. **Make copies** - Keep one for your records

### Submission
1. **Call the bank** to confirm requirements and make an appointment
2. **Gather all required documents** (use the checklist above)
3. **Bring originals and copies** - Bank will keep copies, return originals
4. **Complete bank's application** - May be online or in-person
5. **Wait for account opening** - Usually same-day or next business day

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 🚫 **Fatal Errors (Will Cause Rejection)**
1. **Missing notarization** - Most banks require it
2. **Incorrect company name** - Must match Articles of Organization exactly
3. **Invalid EIN** - Must have IRS CP 575 letter
4. **Not in good standing** - Certificate must be current
5. **Unauthorized signatories** - Must be listed in Operating Agreement

### ⚠️ **Delays and Issues**
1. **Old documents** - Good Standing Certificate > 90 days old
2. **Missing signatures** - All signatories must sign
3. **Incomplete information** - All fields must be filled
4. **No specimen signatures** - Banks need to verify signatures
5. **Wrong entity type** - LLC vs Corporation matters

### 📝 **Minor Issues (Can Usually Be Fixed)**
1. **Typographical errors** - Can be corrected and re-notarized
2. **Missing company seal** - Most banks don't require it
3. **Wrong date format** - Usually acceptable if date is clear
4. **Extra white space** - Doesn't affect validity

## 🔍 How Banks Verify Your Resolution

### Step 1: Initial Review
- Checks that all required fields are completed
- Verifies company name matches other documents
- Confirms notarization is present

### Step 2: Document Cross-Reference
- Compares signatories with Operating Agreement
- Verifies EIN with IRS CP 575
- Checks company status with Secretary of State

### Step 3: Identity Verification
- Validates photo IDs of all signatories
- May run background checks
- Confirms addresses match

### Step 4: Account Opening
- Enters authorized signatories into system
- Sets up signature cards
- Configures account permissions

## 💡 Tips for Success

### **Before Visiting the Bank**
✅ **Call ahead** - Confirm requirements and make an appointment
✅ **Check bank hours** - Many close early on weekends
✅ **Bring all documents** - Use the checklist above
✅ **Dress professionally** - First impressions matter
✅ **Know your business** - Be ready to explain your company

### **During the Bank Visit**
✅ **Be organized** - Have documents in order
✅ **Ask questions** - Clarify anything you don't understand
✅ **Review everything** - Double-check all information
✅ **Get business cards** - For future reference
✅ **Confirm next steps** - When will account be ready?

### **After Account Opening**
✅ **Test your account** - Make a small deposit/withdrawal
✅ **Order checks** - If needed
✅ **Set up online banking** - Usually available immediately
✅ **File with your records** - Keep copies in a safe place
✅ **Update your books** - Record the new account

## 📞 Who to Call for Help

### **Secretary of State**
- Verify company status and good standing
- Obtain certified copies of formation documents
- File annual reports if needed

### **IRS**
- Verify EIN status
- Request copy of CP 575 letter if lost
- Update business information

### **Notary Public**
- Locations: UPS Store, banks, shipping stores, law offices
- Cost: $5-$15 per signature
- Requirements: Valid photo ID

### **Business Attorney**
- Review resolution for completeness
- Advise on complex ownership structures
- Help with bank-specific requirements

## 📊 State-Specific Considerations

### **Delaware**
- No state income tax for LLCs not operating in DE
- Franchise tax due annually
- Many large companies incorporated in DE

### **California**
- LLC tax of $800 annually
- Franchise tax board verification
- Additional local business taxes

### **New York**
- Publication requirement for LLCs in some counties
- Annual filing fee
- State-specific banking regulations

### **Texas**
- No state income tax
- Franchise tax for some entities
- Business-friendly banking environment

### **Florida**
- No state income tax
- Annual report required
- Popular for business formation

**Always check your state's Secretary of State website for current requirements.**

## 🔄 Updating Your Resolution

### **When to Update**
- Adding or removing authorized signatories
- Changing company name or structure
- Moving to a new state
- Opening accounts at a new bank
- Changing signature authority

### **Update Process**
1. **Adopt a new resolution** or amendment
2. **Follow the same execution process** (sign and notarize)
3. **Submit to all banks** where you have accounts
4. **Update your records**
5. **Notify relevant parties**
